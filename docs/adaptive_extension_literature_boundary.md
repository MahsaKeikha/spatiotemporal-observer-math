# Adaptive extension literature boundary

This note records neighboring problem families that must be distinguished from
the Research I extension before any priority claim is made. It is a scientific
scope document, not a venue or submission plan.

## Controlled sensing and sequential testing

Controlled sensing for sequential multihypothesis testing already studies
adaptive selection of measurement actions before a stopping decision. The
Research I extension therefore does not claim novelty for adaptive action
selection, KL-based discrimination, likelihood-ratio accumulation, or stopping
rules in isolation.

Reference:
S. Nitinawarat, G. K. Atia, and V. V. Veeravalli, "Controlled sensing for
sequential multihypothesis testing," IEEE ISIT, 2012.
DOI: 10.1109/ISIT.2012.6283841.

## Dynamic and temporal community tracking

Temporal-network literature already studies communities whose membership
changes through time, including formulations that explicitly describe evolving
communities as tube-like structures. Research I therefore does not claim
novelty merely because its candidate subsystem moves across coordinates.

Representative references:
- M. Vaiana, E. M. Goldberg, and S. F. Muldoon, "Optimizing state change
  detection in functional temporal networks through dynamic community
  detection," Journal of Complex Networks 7(4), 529-553, 2019.
  DOI: 10.1093/comnet/cny030.
- M. T. Schaub et al., "Robust dynamic community detection with applications
  to human brain functional networks," Nature Communications 11, 2020.
  DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-16285-7.
- N. Djurdjevac Conrad et al., "Detection of dynamic communities in temporal
  networks with sparse data," Applied Network Science 10, 1, 2025.
  DOI: 10.1007/s41109-024-00687-3.

## Research I distinction under test

The narrower contribution to evaluate is the complete composition:
1. infer a candidate moving subsystem from stochastic state dynamics;
2. score integration, insulation, persistence, and transport;
3. optimize a finite-horizon world-tube and identify its runner-up;
4. propagate covariance uncertainty into strict path-action separation;
5. refuse singleton identification under observational equivalence;
6. choose additional measurements using retained competitor geometry;
7. keep structural certification and predictive evidence distinct;
8. invalidate and explicitly rebuild only the records affected by assumption
   repair.

No claim is made here that this full composition is absent from all prior
literature. That requires a broader claim-level search across active sensing,
dynamic support/subspace recovery, change-point methods, temporal clustering,
system identification, and finite-sample covariance theory.

## Claim discipline

Any final novelty statement should name the exact mathematical object or
guarantee being compared. Generic ingredients such as mutual information,
conditional mutual information, canonical correlation, Jaccard distance,
dynamic programming, Wishart concentration, KL divergence, likelihood ratios,
and active sensing must be attributed to their established literatures rather
than presented as inventions of this project.
